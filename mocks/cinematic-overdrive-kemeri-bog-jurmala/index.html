The Great Ķemeri Bog has been growing upward for around ten thousand years, older than the Pyramids, fed only by rainwater and building at about a millimetre a year. The peat beneath your feet runs metres deep. On top sits a flat, treeless expanse of soft sphagnum moss, dwarf pines that may be 200 or 300 years old and still no taller than a Christmas tree, and dozens of dark pools that mirror the sky.

We start with a short warm-up on the wooden boardwalk while your guide points out the sundew, the wild rosemary and the dwarf pine, and runs through the safety brief. Then we step onto the open mire. The surface is a floating mat of living moss resting on dark water. With rubber shoes and bogshoes that spread your weight, you walk across it. The ground flexes under each step. It is, almost literally, walking on water. Roughly halfway we reach a small bog lake and stop for a picnic, and anyone who would like a quick dip in the antiseptic, mineral-rich water can take one, with a life vest and guided entry.

The same minerals under this bog built Latvia's spa industry and still turn up in its natural cosmetics, and your guide tells that story, and the ecology behind it, as you walk. We are honest that the entry and exit onto the open bog are physically demanding over uneven moss, and that the bog is a wild place, so we send a short waiver to read in advance. The day runs May to September, when the bog is at its best and the boardwalk is not iced over. At the van afterwards we serve tea, coffee and something sweet while you dry off, then drive you back to Riga.

Your half-day, step by step

1

Hotel pickup in central Riga

We collect you at the start of your chosen slot, early morning for the sunrise hike, mid-day for the gentlest mosquito conditions, or late evening for sunset over the pools. The exact time is confirmed the evening before and depends on the season.

2

Drive west to Ķemeri National Park

About 50 quiet minutes on the road. Your guide uses the drive to set up the day, what a raised bog actually is, why Latvia's peatlands matter on a planetary scale, and what to look for once we are out on the moss.

3

Boardwalk warm-up, then off the boards

A short warm-up on the wooden boardwalk, the safety brief and waiver, then we step onto the open bog in rubber shoes and bogshoes. The entrance is the physically demanding part, uneven and springy. Once you are on the open mire it flattens out and the walking becomes easy.

4

Lake picnic and optional swim

Roughly halfway we reach a small bog lake and stop for a brief picnic on the moss. Anyone who would like a quick swim in the antiseptic, mineral-rich water can, with a life vest for every swimmer and guided entry. Three or four minutes is plenty, around 14 to 18 degrees in summer.

5

Hike back out

Back the way we came, with the light shifting as we walk. On the sunrise and sunset slots we plan the route so the most photogenic stretch lines up with the best light. About two and a half hours on the bog from car park to car park, at a relaxed pace.

6

Refreshments at the van, then back to Riga

Tea, coffee, biscuits, a Latvian pastry, crisps and chocolate at the van while your guide answers any last questions and you warm up and dry off. Then we drive you back to your hotel. Four hours door to door.

Frequently asked questions

What does the off-boardwalk hike actually involve?

We start with a short warm-up on the boardwalk so your guide can introduce the ecosystem, the sphagnum, the sundew, the wild rosemary and the dwarf pine. Then we step off into the open raised bog. You wear our rubber shoes, sized to your booking, plus bogshoes that spread your weight across the moss, and walk a guided loop to a small bog lake. We pause for a brief picnic, anyone who would like a quick swim can, and then we hike back. Around two and a half hours on the bog, four hours from Riga and back.

Is the bog hike physically demanding? Is it dangerous?

It is a soft adventure rather than a serious expedition, but real risk is real risk. The hardest sections are the very start and the very end, getting onto and off the open bog over uneven, springy moss. In the middle the walking is gentle. The bog is a wild place with deep peat and dark pools, so we ask everyone to listen to the guide, follow the route they walk and stay close. By joining you accept the inherent risk, and we send a short waiver to read in advance.

What time slot is best?

Three slots. Early morning is the sunrise hike, the most photogenic and the quietest. Mid-day has the gentlest mosquito conditions and is the easiest to wake up for. Late evening is the sunset hike, equally photogenic but with more mosquitoes, and we provide repellent. The golden-hour slots tend to fill first. You pick when you book.

Tell me about the optional swim. Is the water cold?

The swim is genuinely optional, a quick dip rather than a session, three or four minutes is plenty. The bog lake is small, dark with tannins and naturally antiseptic from the bog acids, the same family of waters that built Latvia's spa industry. We provide a life vest for every swimmer and the entry and exit are guided. The water sits around 14 to 18 degrees in summer. If cold open water is not your thing, give it a miss, nobody is pressured.

What is the difference between bogshoes and rubber shoes?

Rubber shoes are knee-high waterproof boots that go over your socks and keep your feet dry on the wet moss. Bogshoes are wide, lightweight frames, the same idea as snowshoes, that strap on top and spread your weight so you do not sink into the soft sphagnum. You wear both. We need your shoe size when you book so we can guarantee a fit on the rubber shoes.

Is this suitable for children?

Children aged 3 to 14 are welcome at €35. The off-boardwalk hike is more demanding than a normal walk and is not suitable for very young children, so tell us your children's ages and shoe sizes when you book and the guide will plan the pace with the group in mind. The lake swim is a parental judgement call, and life vests are provided for swimmers of all ages.

Are mosquitoes a problem on this tour?

Sometimes, yes, especially on the late evening slot when the bog is at its buggiest. We provide a generic over-the-counter repellent for any guest who would like one, used at your own risk, and long sleeves and trousers help a lot. The mid-day slot has the fewest mosquitoes. If you have sensitive skin, bring your own preferred repellent.
